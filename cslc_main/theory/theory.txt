\begin{abstract}
This document is the theoretical and numerical reference for the
\emph{v2} CSLC contact model implemented in
\texttt{newton/\_src/geometry/cslc\_kernels.py}.  The defining v2
change is that every \emph{(pad sphere, target sample)} pair contacts
via the signed \emph{half-space overlap}
$\mathrm{raw}_{ij} = r_i - \bnf_{\!j}\!\cdot\!(\bq_i - \bt_j)$
(eq.~\eqref{eq:raw}), in place of the v1 sphere-vs-sphere overlap
$(r+R)-\|\bq-\bt\|$ which was non-monotone past face crossing and
required a target sample radius $R$ that has no physical meaning on a
mesh.  This single substitution propagates through every layer of the
model: the contact direction is now the target's outward face normal
(not the line of centres), curved targets are sampled as
\emph{point-set} surfaces with no per-sample radius, and the
distance-preserving lateral law (the v1 source of curvature-driven
Poisson bulging) is replaced by graph-Laplacian throughout, with the
\emph{no-bulge} property of the resulting equilibrium guarded by
regression test T-G.  The unified half-space formulation is verified
against the production Warp kernel by the bridge harness T-K + T-L at
production smoothing $\varepsilon = 5\times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m}$.
A glossary of v1$\to$v2 differences is in
Appendix~\ref{sec:v1-v2-glossary}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Overview and Scope}
\label{sec:overview}

A robot fingertip is approximated by a rigid body wrapped in a layer
of $n$ surface spheres, the \emph{lattice}.  Each surface sphere $i$
has a rest centre $\bp_i\in\real^3$, a rest radius $r_i$, an outward
unit rest normal $\bnp_i$, and a set of \emph{lattice neighbours}
$\mathcal{N}(i)$ (its first ring of edges in the lattice).  When the
body presses into a target, each lattice sphere's centre translates by
a vector displacement $\bdelta_i\in\real^3$, while the rest radius
$r_i$ is kept fixed.  The displaced (\emph{deformed}) centre is
\begin{equation}
  \bq_i \;=\; \bp_i \;-\; \bdelta_i.
  \label{eq:def-centre}
\end{equation}
This convention (centre moves, radius does not) is the
\emph{deformed-centre} formulation.  It is the choice that lets the
anchor and the contact springs sit cleanly in series at the level of a
single sphere (\cref{sec:single}) and carries through to lateral
coupling, anisotropic anchor, and stick--slip friction without any
sign-convention drift between the modules.

\paragraph{Target model (v2).}  A target is \emph{any} surface,
sampled as a discrete set of $M$ \emph{points} carrying outward face
normals and Voronoi areas:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{target} \;=\; \{\,(\bt_j,\ \bnf_{\!j},\ A_j)\,\}_{j=1}^{M},
  \qquad
  \|\bnf_{\!j}\|=1,\ A_j > 0\ [\mathrm{m}^2].
  \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
There is \emph{no per-sample radius}.  The v1 sphere indenter
(\texttt{RigidTarget(t, R)}) is reborn as a Fibonacci-spiral
sampling of the sphere's surface
(\texttt{make\_sphere\_target(t, R, n\_samples)}), with each sample
carrying the radial outward normal as $\bnf_{\!j}$.  Other targets are
flat faces, axis-aligned boxes, and general triangle meshes -- all
samplers live in \texttt{cslc\_main/theory/cslc\_targets.py} and
return a \texttt{PointSetTargetV2} dataclass.  This unification is the
\emph{point-set abstraction}: any shape becomes a point cloud with
face normals, and the same contact code runs against all of them.

\paragraph{Half-space contact (v2).}  The pad sphere $i$ contacts the
target sample $j$ via the signed half-space overlap
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{raw}_{ij}(\bdelta_i)
  \;=\; r_i \;-\; \bnf_{\!j}\!\cdot\!(\bq_i - \bt_j).
  \label{eq:raw}
\end{equation}
The contact direction is the \emph{target face normal} $\bnf_{\!j}$
(not the line of centres $(\bq_i-\bt_j)/\|\cdot\|$).  Three properties
follow directly:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Monotonicity at any depth.}  Fix $\bnf_{\!j}$ and
$\bt_j$; let $\bq_i$ vary along the $-\bnf_{\!j}$ direction (deeper into the
target).  Then $\bnf_{\!j}\!\cdot\!(\bq_i-\bt_j)$ strictly
\emph{decreases}, so $\mathrm{raw}_{ij}$ strictly \emph{increases}.
There is no sign flip; deep-penetration is well-defined.  Regression
guarded by T-A (\texttt{test\_half\_space\_monotonicity.py}) and T-B
(\texttt{test\_half\_space\_deep\_penetration.py}).
\item \emph{Face-defined contact direction.}  The contact force is
along $\pm\bnf_{\!j}$, regardless of where $\bq_i$ sits relative to
$\bt_j$.  Closing along the face normal means a pad sphere whose
centre has crossed the face still experiences a force that points
\emph{out of the body}; the v1 line-of-centres form sign-flipped at
face crossing.
\item \emph{No target sample radius $R$.}  Every term in \eqref{eq:raw}
is a property of either the pad ($r_i$) or the target's local
surface geometry ($\bnf_{\!j}, \bt_j$).  The v1 ``$R$'' was a
discretisation hack ($R=\mathrm{pad\_spacing}/2$ on a mesh); v2 drops
it entirely.
\end{enumerate}

Three forces act on each surface lattice sphere: the \emph{anchor}
(\cref{sec:anchor}), pulling $\bq_i$ back toward $\bp_i$; the
\emph{lateral coupling} (\cref{sec:lateral}), graph-Laplacian springs
that connect $\bq_i$ to its lattice neighbours; and the
\emph{contact} sum (\cref{sec:contact}) over active target samples.
Stick--slip friction (\cref{sec:friction}) is added as a fourth term
acting in the tangent plane of $\bnp_i$ (the \emph{pad's} outward
normal, contract \S6.4).  Every gate that would otherwise introduce
non-differentiability is replaced by a $C^{\infty}$ surrogate
parameterised by a single smoothing width $\varepsilon$
(\cref{sec:smooth-surrogates}).

\paragraph{What this document supersedes.}  v1 included a
\emph{distance-preserving} lateral law on edge length and a curvature-
driven \emph{Poisson bulge} phenomenon as physical predictions.  Both
are deleted in v2: the only lateral law is graph-Laplacian, and the
\emph{absence} of bulge is a regression-guarded property of the model
(T-G, \texttt{test\_dome\_flattens.py}).  See
Appendix~\ref{sec:v1-v2-glossary} for the full v1$\to$v2 diff.

\paragraph{Sign conventions.}  Throughout this document, $\delta_n>0$
means the sphere has been compressed \emph{inward}, i.e.\ $\bq_i$ lies
on the \emph{body} side of $\bp_i$ along the pad's outward normal
$\bnp_i$.  Because $\bq_i=\bp_i-\bdelta_i$ the chain rule contributes
a $-1$ when converting between $\partial/\partial\bq_i$ and
$\partial/\partial\bdelta_i$, so the physical force on the sphere
$\bef^{\mathrm{phys}}_i=-\partial E/\partial\bq_i=+\partial
E/\partial\bdelta_i$ coincides in direction with the energy gradient
with respect to $\bdelta_i$.  We therefore work in terms of
$\partial E/\partial\bdelta_i$ throughout, and write the equilibrium
directly as
$\partial E_{\mathrm{tot}}/\partial\bdelta_i=0$ (sum of gradient
contributions equals zero).  The Warp kernel names its per-iteration
force quantities \emph{loads}, defined as $-\partial
E_X/\partial\bdelta$; this is the same physics with the opposite sign
convention, and we flag the sign flip explicitly when we cite the
kernel.  These conventions are collected in \cref{tab:signs}.

\section{Geometry primitives}
\label{sec:primitives}

\subsection{Half-space raw overlap}

For pad sphere $i$ at displacement $\bdelta_i$, the deformed centre is
$\bq_i = \bp_i - \bdelta_i$, and the (signed) half-space overlap with
target sample $j$ is~\eqref{eq:raw}.  The \emph{rest} overlap is the
value at $\bdelta_i = 0$:
\begin{equation}
  d_{ij}^{\mathrm{rest}}
  \;=\; r_i \;-\; \bnf_{\!j}\!\cdot\!(\bp_i - \bt_j).
  \label{eq:raw-rest}
\end{equation}
Code: \texttt{half\_space\_raw}, \texttt{half\_space\_phi\_eff}
(\texttt{cslc\_theory.py}).

\paragraph{Monotonicity.}  For fixed $(\bnf_{\!j}, \bt_j)$ and any
sphere-translation $\bdelta_i \to \bdelta_i + t\,\bnp_i$ that moves
$\bq_i$ \emph{into} the target body (along $-\bnf_{\!j}$ when
$\bnp_i$ and $\bnf_{\!j}$ are anti-parallel),
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{\partial\,\mathrm{raw}_{ij}}{\partial\bdelta_i}
  \;=\; -\,\bnf_{\!j}\!\cdot\!\frac{\partial\bq_i}{\partial\bdelta_i}
  \;=\; +\,\bnf_{\!j},
\end{equation*}
so $\mathrm{raw}$ grows linearly with the penetration depth, without
sign flip at $\bq_i = \bt_j$ (the v1 cliff).  This is the
defining property of the half-space form; it is the regression target
of T-A and T-B.

\subsection{Tangential distance and locality kernel}

The tangential displacement of $\bq_i$ relative to sample $j$ in the
face plane is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{d}^{t}_{ij}
  \;=\; (\bq_i - \bt_j) \;-\;
        \bigl[\bnf_{\!j}\!\cdot\!(\bq_i - \bt_j)\bigr]\,\bnf_{\!j},
  \qquad
  d^{t}_{ij} \;=\; \|\mathbf{d}^{t}_{ij}\|.
  \label{eq:dt}
\end{equation}
The \emph{tangential locality kernel} caps each pad sphere's reach to
a few pad radii in the face plane:
\begin{equation}
  w^{t}_{ij}
  \;=\; \Sigma_{\varepsilon}\!\bigl(3 r_i - d^{t}_{ij}\bigr),
  \qquad
  \Sigma_{\varepsilon}(x) =
  \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1 + x/\sqrt{x^2+\varepsilon^2}\bigr).
  \label{eq:w-t}
\end{equation}
Kernel half-width $3 r_i$ exceeds typical pad spacing so the contact
patch is sampled by several target points; below that $w^{t}$ falls
smoothly to zero and prevents a pad sphere from coupling to far-away
samples (e.g.\ samples on the opposite face of a box).

\subsection{Smooth surrogates}
\label{sec:smooth-surrogates}

Two $C^{\infty}$ surrogates replace the hard ReLU and Heaviside:
\begin{align}
  \sigma_{\varepsilon}(x)
  &\;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(x + \sqrt{x^2 + \varepsilon^2}\bigr),
  &&\text{(smooth ReLU; }\sigma_{\varepsilon}\to[x]_+\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0\text{)}
  \label{eq:sigma}\\
  \Sigma_{\varepsilon}(x)
  &\;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(1 + \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+\varepsilon^2}}\right),
  &&\text{(smooth Heaviside; }\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\to\Theta(x)\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0\text{)}
  \label{eq:Sigma}
\end{align}
both with pointwise error bounded by $\varepsilon/2$, and satisfying
the chain-rule identity $\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{\prime} =
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}$.

The active-set quantities at each $(i, j)$ pair are then
\begin{equation}
  \phi^{\mathrm{eff}}_{ij}
  \;=\; \sigma_{\varepsilon}\bigl(\mathrm{raw}_{ij}\bigr),
  \qquad
  \mathrm{gate}_{ij}
  \;=\; \Sigma_{\varepsilon}\bigl(\mathrm{raw}_{ij}\bigr).
  \label{eq:phi-eff-and-gate}
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Production default.}  $\varepsilon = 5\times 10^{-4}\,$m
makes the gate effectively binary above $5\,$mm of penetration while
keeping the contact force $C^{\infty}$ in the rigid-body pose.

\subsection{Active-set culling}
\label{sec:active-set}

A pair $(i, j)$ is \emph{inactive} and may be skipped without
numerical error iff \emph{either}
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{raw}_{ij} \;<\; -50\,\varepsilon
  \qquad\text{(depth gate),}
  \label{eq:inactive}
\end{equation}
or the face normals are not opposing,
\begin{equation}
  -\,\bnf_{\!j}\!\cdot\!\bnp_i \;\le\; -\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{align}}
  \qquad\text{(alignment gate, contract \S3.6).}
  \label{eq:align-cull}
\end{equation}
The constant $-50$ is \texttt{INACTIVE\_RAW\_EPS\_FACTOR} in
\texttt{cslc\_theory.py} and is hand-copied as the literal
\texttt{-50.0 * eps} at each kernel site; the constant-discipline
guard in \texttt{test\_07\_kernel\_bridge.py} regression-tests both
parts (magnitude match and adjacent comment naming the symbol) at
every commit.  Similarly $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{align}} = 0.05$ is
\texttt{EPS\_ALIGN\_DEFAULT} in \texttt{cslc\_theory.py} and is
hand-copied as the literal \texttt{0.05} in
\texttt{jacobi\_step\_point\_set}; the same guard regression-tests
this literal in parallel.

\paragraph{Why the alignment gate.}  The half-space form alone gives
spurious ``contact'' on the \emph{far} side of a closed convex body
(sphere, box, mesh) -- the pad's deformed centre is geometrically
inside the half-plane of every far-side sample, but those samples'
outward face normals point \emph{away} from the pad and represent
surfaces facing away.  v1 implicitly culled them via the 3-D distance
gate $(r+R)-\|\bq-\bt\| < -50\varepsilon$, but with $R=0$ that gate
collapses; the alignment gate is the v2 replacement.  Letting
$\alpha_{ij} \equiv -\,\bnf_{\!j}\!\cdot\!\bnp_i$ ($+1$ at perfect
face-on, $-1$ at back-to-back), the smooth gate is
\begin{equation}
  a_{ij}
  \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    1, & \alpha_{ij} \ge +\varepsilon_{\mathrm{align}},\\
    t^{2}(3-2t),\ \ t = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\dfrac{\alpha_{ij}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{align}}}{2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{align}}},0,1\right),
       & |\alpha_{ij}| < \varepsilon_{\mathrm{align}},\\
    0, & \alpha_{ij} \le -\varepsilon_{\mathrm{align}}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:align-gate}
\end{equation}
This is a $C^{1}$ cubic smoothstep, exactly $1$ on face-on, exactly
$0$ on back-to-back, with compact support and \emph{no polynomial
tail} -- back-side pairs contribute mathematically zero.  $a_{ij}$ is
$\bdelta$-independent (both normals are rest body-local), so the
gradient picks up no extra $\partial a_{ij}/\partial\bdelta$ term;
$a_{ij}$ enters every force/energy expression as a constant
multiplier.

\paragraph{Cull-product magnitude check.}  At the depth threshold
$\mathrm{raw} = -50\,\varepsilon$,
$\phi^{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 0.005\,\varepsilon$ and
$\mathrm{gate} \approx 1.0\times 10^{-4}$ \emph{individually}; neither is
small on its own.  What justifies the cull is the \emph{product} that
enters every force / energy term:
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{\varepsilon}(-50\varepsilon)\;\cdot\;\Sigma_{\varepsilon}(-50\varepsilon)
  \;\approx\; 5\times 10^{-7}\,\varepsilon.
  \label{eq:cull-product}
\end{equation}
At production $\varepsilon = 5\times 10^{-4}\,$m this product is
$2.5\times 10^{-10}\,$m.  Multiplied by typical
$k_c\,A_j\,w^{t} \sim 10^{-2}\,$N$/$m at production sampling density,
the per-pair force contribution from a culled pair is
$\sim 10^{-11}\,$N -- well below the $1$--$100\,$N range of total
contact wrench, so the cull is numerically harmless.

\paragraph{Phase 4b amendment notice.}  For closed convex targets
whose side faces are flat extensions of the contact face (canonical
example: an axis-aligned box pressing one face against a pad), corner
samples on the perpendicular side faces sit at $\alpha_{ij} = 0$, hit
the middle of the smooth band, and contribute at $a_{ij} = 0.5$.  This
is not zero -- the pad receives asymmetric in-plane forces from
geometric configurations the contract intends to cull.  Phase 4b will
shift the smooth band from $[-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{align}},
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{align}}]$ to
$[0, +\varepsilon_{\mathrm{align}}]$ (perpendicular hard-culled with a
small smoothing band near face-on for angular jitter).  Phase 4a's
T-K scene H uses a single-face point set to side-step this until the
amendment lands.

\subsection{Verification (T-A, T-B)}

\textbf{T-A} (\texttt{test\_half\_space\_monotonicity.py}) sweeps a
single pad sphere from $\bq_i$ at $+10 r_i$ outside a flat face to
$-10 r_i$ inside the body and verifies (a) $\mathrm{raw}(\bq_i)$
strictly increasing; (b) $\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}(\bq_i)$ non-decreasing;
(c) $\mathrm{gate}(\bq_i)$ non-decreasing; (d) no sign flip.
\textbf{T-B} (\texttt{test\_half\_space\_deep\_penetration.py}) drives
$\bq_i$ to $-10 r_i$ \emph{inside} the body and verifies the force is
finite, monotone, has $\mathrm{gate}=1$ (saturated), and contains no
NaN -- v1's $(r+R)-\|\cdot\|$ form would drop to zero past the $r+R$
cliff and reverse sign beyond it.  Both pass to fp precision;
\textbf{Fig.~\ref{fig:tatb}}.

\section{Single-pair contact}
\label{sec:single-pair}

For one (pad sphere $i$, target sample $j$) pair, the contact energy
contribution is
\begin{equation}
  E_{ij}(\bdelta_i)
  \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,k_c\,A_j\,w^{t}_{ij}\,a_{ij}\,
        \bigl[\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}_{ij}\bigr]^{2}.
  \label{eq:E-ij}
\end{equation}
The chain-rule gradient with respect to $\bdelta_i$ uses
$\partial\bq_i/\partial\bdelta_i = -\bI$ and
$\partial\,\mathrm{raw}_{ij}/\partial\bdelta_i = +\bnf_{\!j}$:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial E_{ij}}{\partial\bdelta_i}
  \;=\; k_c\,A_j\,w^{t}_{ij}\,a_{ij}\,
        \phi^{\mathrm{eff}}_{ij}\,\mathrm{gate}_{ij}\,\bnf_{\!j}
  \quad\Bigl(\!= +\,\bef^{\mathrm{phys}}_{ij}\Bigr).
  \label{eq:contact-grad}
\end{equation}
The corresponding \emph{kernel-side load} is
$\bef^{\mathrm{load}}_{ij} = -\partial E_{ij}/\partial\bdelta_i$, i.e.\
the same magnitude in the opposite direction; the kernel writes
$\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{eff}} = -\bnf_{\!j}$ (kernel L1516) so its
contact-load formula
$k_c\,A_j\,w^{t}\,a\,\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}\,\mathrm{gate}\,\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{eff}}$
matches the gradient form when both signs are accounted for.

\paragraph{Why $\mathrm{gate}_{ij}$ must appear in the gradient.}
Because $\phi^{\mathrm{eff}} = \sigma_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{raw})$, the
chain rule gives
$\partial[\tfrac{1}{2}(\phi^{\mathrm{eff}})^2]/\partial\bdelta
= \phi^{\mathrm{eff}}\,\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\mathrm{raw})\,\bnf
= \phi^{\mathrm{eff}}\,\Sigma_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{raw})\,\bnf$.
Forgetting $\Sigma_{\varepsilon}$ -- the v1 silent-gradient bug -- is
invisible at deep saturated contact
($\mathrm{raw}\gg\varepsilon$, $\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\approx 1$) but is
up to a factor of $2$ wrong at contact onset
($\mathrm{raw}\sim\varepsilon$), and on non-contact pairs
($\mathrm{raw}\ll -\varepsilon$) it leaks a spurious
$k_c\,\varepsilon/2$ load because $\sigma_{\varepsilon}$ has an
irreducible $\varepsilon/2$ floor while $\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\to 0$
kills the leak.

\paragraph{$w^{t}$-derivative status (Phase 4a commit).}  Strictly,
$w^{t}_{ij}$ is also $\bdelta$-dependent through $d^{t}_{ij}$, so the
chain rule has an extra term
$\tfrac{1}{2}k_c A_j a_{ij}(\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}_{ij})^{2}\,
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(3r_i - d^{t}_{ij})\,
\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{ij}$, with
$\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{ij} = \mathbf{d}^{t}_{ij}/d^{t}_{ij}$.  Both the
production Warp kernel \emph{and} the theory's
\texttt{solve\_lattice\_contact} drop this term: the contribution
scales as $O(\varepsilon^{2}/r_{i}^{3})$ -- about $1.4\,$m$^{-1}$ at
production $\varepsilon=5\times 10^{-4}\,$m, $r_i = 1.5\times 10^{-3}\,$m
-- well below L-BFGS-B's converged gradient floor of $\sim 5\times
10^{-8}$ (\cref{sec:lessons} \#1).  Theory $=$ kernel ``never both
ways''; this commit was made in Phase 4a as part of building the
bridge harness against a consistent operator on both sides.

\section{Total contact on a pad sphere}
\label{sec:contact}

Per pad sphere $i$, sum the per-pair gradient over all target samples:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial}{\partial\bdelta_i}\!\sum_{j} E_{ij}
  \;=\; \sum_{j}
        k_c\,A_j\,w^{t}_{ij}\,a_{ij}\,
        \phi^{\mathrm{eff}}_{ij}\,\mathrm{gate}_{ij}\,\bnf_{\!j}.
  \label{eq:F-contact-i}
\end{equation}
Active-set culling \eqref{eq:inactive}-\eqref{eq:align-cull} restricts
the sum to nearby, opposing-face pairs.

\paragraph{Per-sphere normal force read-out.}  The friction model
(\cref{sec:friction}) and the grip aggregation T-J both need the
scalar normal force at each pad sphere, defined as the magnitude of
the projection of \eqref{eq:F-contact-i} on the pad's own outward
normal:
\begin{equation}
  f^{n}_{i}
  \;=\;
  \left|\,
    \biggl(\sum_{j}
        k_c\,A_j\,w^{t}_{ij}\,a_{ij}\,
        \phi^{\mathrm{eff}}_{ij}\,\mathrm{gate}_{ij}\,\bnf_{\!j}\biggr)
    \!\cdot\!\bnp_i
  \,\right|.
  \label{eq:fn-i}
\end{equation}
Implementation: \texttt{lattice\_contact\_normal\_forces}
(\texttt{cslc\_lattice.py}), which evaluates the same expression as
\eqref{eq:F-contact-i} at a given equilibrium $\bdelta$.

\section{Single pad sphere: equilibrium against a flat face}
\label{sec:single}

\subsection{Closed form, face-on}

Take a single pad sphere with isotropic anchor $k_a$ and a single
target sample $(\bt, \bnf_{\!})$ with $\bnf_{\!} = -\bnp$ (face-on).
The rest overlap \eqref{eq:raw-rest} reduces to
$d = r - \bnf_{\!}\!\cdot\!(\bp-\bt)$.  Symmetry forces
$\bdelta = \delta_n \bnp$, and the scalar equilibrium
$\partial E_{\mathrm{tot}}/\partial\delta_n = 0$ collapses to
\begin{equation}
  k_a\,\delta_n \;-\; k_c\,(d - \delta_n) \;=\; 0
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \delta_n^{*}
  \;=\;
  \frac{k_c\,d}{k_a + k_c},
  \qquad
  F^{*} \;=\; \frac{k_a\,k_c}{k_a+k_c}\,d
        \;=\; k_{\mathrm{eff}}\,d.
  \label{eq:series-2}
\end{equation}
This is the textbook two-spring series law (anchor in series with
contact), and is the v2 successor of v1 \texttt{equilibrium\_face\_on}-
\texttt{\_analytical}.  Code:
\texttt{equilibrium\_half\_space\_face\_on\_analytical}.

\subsection{Off-axis tilt with anisotropic anchor}
\label{sec:off-axis}

Tilt the target's face by angle $\theta$ relative to the pad's normal,
so $\bnf_{\!} \cdot \bnp = -\cos\theta$.  With anisotropic anchor
\eqref{eq:anchor-aniso} (tangent ratio $\rho$), the equilibrium splits
into normal/tangent components.  Because $\partial\,\mathrm{raw}/\partial\bdelta
= +\bnf$ is \emph{constant} in $\bdelta$ (v2 face normal is $\bdelta$-
independent), the tangent ratio simplifies to the exact closed form
\begin{equation}
  \left|\frac{\delta_t^{*}}{\delta_n^{*}}\right|
  \;=\; \frac{1}{\rho}\,\tan\theta.
  \label{eq:aniso-tilt}
\end{equation}
The v1 form
$|{\delta_t}/{\delta_n}| = \frac{k_a - A}{k_a\rho - A}\,\tan\theta$
with $A = k_c\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}/L$ was wrong by up to $21\%$ at
$\rho = 0.1$ -- the v1 line-of-centres direction rotated with
$\bdelta$, producing the $A$ correction; in v2 that rotation does not
occur and the correction vanishes.

\subsection{Verification (T-C, T-D)}

\textbf{T-C} (\texttt{test\_face\_on\_series\_spring.py}) verifies
\eqref{eq:series-2} on a 15-cell $(d, k_c/k_a)$ grid: numerical
L-BFGS-B on $E_{\mathrm{tot}}$ matches the closed form to
$1.1\times 10^{-19}\,$m absolute / $1.4\times 10^{-14}\,$N at
$\varepsilon = 1\times 10^{-9}\,$m (theory grade).  Part D
quantifies the \emph{production-eps precision floor}: at production
$\varepsilon = 5\times 10^{-4}\,$m the smooth-surrogate force matches
analytical to $<1\%$ when $\mathrm{raw}_{\mathrm{eq}} \ge 10\,\varepsilon$,
and the rel-err scales $\sim 1/(\mathrm{raw}_{\mathrm{eq}}/\varepsilon)^{4}$
in the leading correction.  This is the precision budget every

\textbf{T-D} (\texttt{test\_tilted\_face.py}) verifies the tilt
formula \eqref{eq:aniso-tilt} on a $\rho \times \theta$ grid with
$\rho \in \{1, 1/2, 1/3, 0.1\}$ and $\theta \in \{5^{\circ}, 15^{\circ},

\section{Lattice equilibrium}
\label{sec:lattice}

The total energy on a lattice of $n$ pad spheres is
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{tot}}(\bdelta_{1\!:\!n})
  \;=\;
  \sum_{i}\!E_{\mathrm{anchor},i}
  \;+\; E_{\mathrm{lat}}
  \;+\;
  \sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\!\sum_{j}\!
       \tfrac{1}{2}\,k_c\,A_j\,w^{t}_{ij}\,a_{ij}\,
       \bigl[\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}_{ij}\bigr]^{2}
  \;+\;
  \sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\!E^{(i)}_{\mathrm{fric}},
  \label{eq:E-tot}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{S}$ is the set of surface pad spheres (those that
participate in contact).  The quasistatic equilibrium is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial E_{\mathrm{tot}}}{\partial\bdelta_i}\bigg|_{i} = 0,
  \qquad \forall i.
  \label{eq:eq-cond}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Anchor (anisotropic)}
\label{sec:anchor}

In each pad sphere's local $\{\bnp_i,\bnp_i^{\perp}\}$ frame, with
$\delta_{n,i} = \bdelta_i\!\cdot\!\bnp_i$ and
$\bdelta_{t,i} = \bdelta_i - \delta_{n,i}\bnp_i$,
\begin{align}
  E_{\mathrm{anchor},i}
  &\;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,k_a\,\delta_{n,i}^{\,2}
       \;+\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,(k_a\,\rho)\,\|\bdelta_{t,i}\|^{2},
  \label{eq:anchor-aniso}\\
  \frac{\partial E_{\mathrm{anchor},i}}{\partial\bdelta_i}
  &\;=\; k_a\,\delta_{n,i}\,\bnp_i \;+\; (k_a\,\rho)\,\bdelta_{t,i}.
  \label{eq:anchor-grad}
\end{align}
$\rho = k_{a,t}/k_a$ is the \emph{tangent ratio}; $\rho = 1$ recovers
the isotropic Hookean spring, and the incompressible flesh limit
$\nu \to 1/2$ gives $\rho = 1/(2(1+\nu)) \to 1/3$.

Because $\bnp_i$ is $\bdelta$-independent (it is the pad sphere's
\emph{rest} outward normal), the gradient picks up no extra
$\partial\bnp_i/\partial\bdelta$ term -- the local frame is fixed by
the rest geometry, not by the current displacement.  Phase 4a's
\texttt{solve\_lattice\_contact} accepts the anisotropic ratio as a
scalar kwarg \texttt{ka\_t\_ratio} so bridge scenes can sweep $\rho$
without rebuilding the \texttt{Lattice} dataclass; the kernel takes
\texttt{ka\_tangent\_ratio} via the same convention.

\subsection{Lateral (graph-Laplacian only, v2)}
\label{sec:lateral}

The conference paper~\cite{v1conf} wrote a distance-preserving
lateral law on edge length; v2 \emph{removes} that law and keeps the
graph-Laplacian form only:
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{lat}}^{\mathrm{GL}}
  \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,k_{\ell}\!\!\sum_{(i,j)\in E}\!
        \bigl\|\bdelta_i - \bdelta_j\bigr\|^{2},
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial E_{\mathrm{lat}}^{\mathrm{GL}}}{\partial\bdelta_i}
  \;=\; k_{\ell}\!\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}\!(\bdelta_i - \bdelta_j).
  \label{eq:lat-GL}
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Consequence: no Poisson bulge.}  The distance-preserving
law had a curvature-driven \emph{bulge window} in which a perimeter
neighbour of the apex on a curved patch developed $\delta_n < 0$
(outward bulge along its own normal) over a window of apex compressions
$0 < \delta_{\mathrm{apex}} < R_{\mathrm{pad}}\,\alpha^{2}$, where
$\alpha$ is the angular spacing.  The graph-Laplacian law has no such
window: it sees only $\bdelta_i - \bdelta_j$, with no awareness of
edge geometry, and always spreads load with the \emph{same} sign as
the loaded sphere.  Curved pad lattices still flatten against a target
(this is a kinematic consequence of the anchor + contact balance), but
no perimeter sphere develops $\delta_n < 0$ under static contact.
The \emph{no-bulge} property is regression-guarded by T-G; see
\cref{sec:T-G}.

\paragraph{Linearised stiffness matrix.}  Linearising
\eqref{eq:E-tot} about $\bdelta = 0$ on the normal axis (face-on
contact) gives the scalar lattice stiffness matrix
\begin{equation}
  K_{ii} \;=\; k_{a} + k_{\ell}\,|\mathcal{N}(i)|,
  \qquad
  K_{ij} \;=\; -k_{\ell}\ \text{if } j\in\mathcal{N}(i),
  \label{eq:Kmatrix}
\end{equation}
SPD for $k_a > 0$; full vector system $(\bK\otimes\bI_3)\,\bdelta =
\bef^{\mathrm{ext}}$ decouples per Cartesian axis.  Code:
\texttt{build\_K\_matrix}.

\subsection{Chain spectrum and Green's function}

For an $N$-sphere chain (degree $1$ at the endpoints, degree $2$
interior), $\bK$ is tridiagonal with closed-form eigenvalues
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_k \;=\; k_a \;+\; 2 k_{\ell}\bigl(1-\cos(k\pi/N)\bigr),
  \qquad k = 0,1,\ldots,N-1,
  \label{eq:chain-spectrum}
\end{equation}
mode $k = 0$ being uniform translation with eigenvalue $\lambda_0 = k_a$
-- the only mode the lateral spring cannot resist.  The discrete
Green's function for a point source at sphere $0$ decays as
$g_i \propto z_{-}^{|i|}$ with
\begin{equation}
  z_{-} \;=\; (1+\alpha) - \sqrt{\alpha(\alpha+2)},
  \qquad
  \alpha \;=\; \frac{k_a}{2 k_{\ell}},
  \qquad
  \ell_{c}^{\mathrm{disc}} \;=\; -\,\frac{1}{\ln z_{-}}.
  \label{eq:chain-decay}
\end{equation}
The continuum approximation $\ell_c = \sqrt{k_{\ell}/k_a}$ is only
accurate at $k_{\ell} \gg k_a$; at the production default
$k_{\ell}/k_a = 0.2$ the discrete decay is $0.520$ spacings (continuum
$0.447$).  Verified by T-E (\texttt{test\_02\_chain.py}):
\textbf{Fig.~\ref{fig:te}}.

\subsection{Chain $+$ flat-face contact (T-F)}

A single flat-face sample placed face-on above sphere $k$ of the chain
inserts a $+k_c\,e_k e_k^{\top}$ rank-1 update on the normal axis.
Sherman--Morrison gives the closed form
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{n,k} \;=\; \frac{k_c\,d\,g_{kk}}{1 + k_c\,g_{kk}},
  \qquad g_{kk} = (\bK^{-1})_{kk},
  \label{eq:chain-SM}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the single-sphere series-spring law \eqref{eq:series-2} with
$k_a$ replaced by the lattice-effective $1/g_{kk}$ at the contact
sphere.  Lateral coupling stiffens the centre's response because
neighbours share the load.  Force balance: $\sum_{i} k_a\,|\delta_i|
= k_c\,(d - \delta_{n,k})$ (Newton III, expressed through the
equilibrium state).  Code:
\texttt{solve\_lattice\_contact} on a chain lattice with a one-sample
\texttt{PointSetTargetV2}, $\mathrm{kernel\_half\_width} < h$ to
restrict contact to the centre pad sphere.  T-F
(\texttt{test\_chain\_contact\_flat.py}) verifies at three
$k_{\ell}/k_a$ and three $k_c/k_a$ ratios with worst rel-err
$5.2\times 10^{-8}$ (the L-BFGS-B floor at this scene scale; the
direct-linear reference inside the same test agrees with S--M to fp
precision).  \textbf{Fig.~\ref{fig:tf}}.

\subsection{Solver algorithm}

The kernel solves \eqref{eq:eq-cond} by damped Jacobi with implicit
diagonal stabilisation.  Per-sphere split into local
$(\delta_{n,i}, \bdelta_{t,i})$ in the pad's rest frame, with
\begin{align*}
  (k_a + S_n)\,\delta_{n,i}^{(k+1)}
  &\;=\; \mathrm{rhs}_{n,i} \;+\; S_n\,\delta_{n,i}^{(k)},
  \\
  (k_a\rho + S_t)\,\bdelta_{t,i}^{(k+1)}
  &\;=\; \mathrm{rhs}_{t,i} \;+\; S_t\,\bdelta_{t,i}^{(k)},
\end{align*}
where $S_n = k_{\ell}|\mathcal{N}(i)| + k_c\sum_{j} A_j w^{t}_{ij}
a_{ij}\,\mathrm{gate}_{ij}$ and $S_t = k_{\ell}|\mathcal{N}(i)|$ are
diagonal contraction bounds, and $\mathrm{rhs}$ is the explicit
per-iteration residual (lateral + contact + friction + external
load).  The update is damped by $\alpha \in (0, 1)$:
\begin{equation}
  \bdelta_i^{(k+1)}
  \;\leftarrow\;
  (1-\alpha)\,\bdelta_i^{(k)} + \alpha\,\bdelta_i^{\mathrm{Jacobi}}.
  \label{eq:jacobi-damp}
\end{equation}
$\alpha = 0.3$ converges in $40$--$100$ sweeps on production grasp
scenes; sharp contacts and near-slip friction take up to $\sim
2000$.  The theory-side reference, \texttt{solve\_lattice\_contact},
runs L-BFGS-B on the same energy \eqref{eq:E-tot} and converges to
the same fixed point up to the documented gradient floor (see
\cref{sec:lessons} \#1).

\section{Targets and samplers}
\label{sec:targets}

A target is a \texttt{PointSetTargetV2} dataclass in
\texttt{cslc\_main/theory/cslc\_targets.py}.  Four concrete samplers
populate the abstract \eqref{eq:target}:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=*]
\item[\texttt{make\_flat\_face\_target(centre, normal, span\_u,
span\_v, pitch)}.]  Regular grid on a flat face;
$M = (\lceil\mathrm{span\_u}/\mathrm{pitch}\rceil + 1)\times
(\lceil\mathrm{span\_v}/\mathrm{pitch}\rceil + 1)$ samples; all carry
the same face normal; $A_j = (\mathrm{span\_u}\,
\mathrm{span\_v})/M$ uniformly.

\item[\texttt{make\_sphere\_target(t, R, n\_samples)}.]
Fibonacci-spiral on the sphere $S^{2}_{R}(\bt)$; each sample's normal
is the radial outward direction at the sample point; $A_j = 4\pi
R^{2}/n_{\mathrm{samples}}$.  The half-space form locally replaces the
sphere with its tangent plane at each sample; the resulting
geometric error in $\mathrm{raw}_{ij}$ is bounded by the sagitta
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{geom}}(d^{t}) = (d^{t})^{2}/(2R)$; the
worst-case relative error per pair is $\sim r_{\mathrm{pad}}^{2}/(R\,d)
\approx 13\%$ at the kernel's lateral edge and much smaller for
nearer samples (the area-weighted sum suppresses the edge-pair
contribution).  Fit-for-purpose at $R \gg r_{\mathrm{pad}}^{2}/d$;
verified on the tennis-ball target ($R = 33.5\,$mm) by T-H.

\item[\texttt{make\_box\_target(extents, n\_samples, *, center, seed)}.]
Per-face area-weighted random sampling of an axis-aligned box via
\texttt{trimesh}; each sample carries its source face's outward
normal; $A_j = \mathrm{total\_box\_surface\_area}/n_{\mathrm{samples}}$
uniformly.

\item[\texttt{make\_mesh\_target(mesh, n\_samples, *, seed)}.]  Generic
\texttt{trimesh} sampler; each sample's normal is the source
triangle's outward normal; $A_j = \mathrm{mesh.area}/n_{\mathrm{samples}}$
(uniform-density approximation).
\end{description}
There is \emph{no per-sample radius} on any target -- the v1
\texttt{radii} field of \texttt{PointSetTarget} is dropped in v2 and
the kernel signature will lose \texttt{target\_radii} in Phase 5.

\section{Stick--slip friction}
\label{sec:friction}

Friction is added as a fourth gradient term, acting in the tangent
plane of the \emph{pad's} outward normal $\bnp_i$ (contract \S6.4).
In the local tangent frame let $s_i = \|\bdelta_{t,i}\|$.  The
\emph{normal force} entering the cone is the magnitude of the
projection of the per-sphere contact-force sum \eqref{eq:F-contact-i}
on $\bnp_i$:
\begin{equation}
  f^{n}_{i}
  \;=\;
  \left|\,\biggl(\sum_{j} k_c A_j w^{t}_{ij} a_{ij}
              \phi^{\mathrm{eff}}_{ij} \mathrm{gate}_{ij} \bnf_{\!j}\biggr)
  \!\cdot\! \bnp_i\,\right|.
  \label{eq:fn-i-bis}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Hard piecewise law}

Letting $k_{a,t} = k_a\rho$, the hard piecewise stick--slip law writes
\begin{align}
  \text{stick }(|F| \le F_{\mathrm{thresh}}):\
  s_i &= \frac{|F|}{k_{a,t} + k_{\mathrm{stick}}},
  & F^{i}_{\mathrm{fric}} &= k_{\mathrm{stick}}\,s_i,
  \label{eq:stick}\\
  \text{slip }(|F| > F_{\mathrm{thresh}}):\
  s_i &= \frac{|F| - \mu\,f^{n}_{i}}{k_{a,t}},
  & F^{i}_{\mathrm{fric}} &= \mu\,f^{n}_{i},
  \label{eq:slip}\\
  F_{\mathrm{thresh}}
  &= \mu\,f^{n}_{i}\,\frac{k_{a,t} + k_{\mathrm{stick}}}{k_{\mathrm{stick}}},
  & & &
  \label{eq:Fthresh}
\end{align}
where $|F| = \|\bef^{\mathrm{ext}}_{t,i}\|$ is the magnitude of the
applied tangential load.  Limits: $k_{\mathrm{stick}}\to\infty$
recovers bare Coulomb ($F_{\mathrm{thresh}}\to\mu f_n$);
$k_{\mathrm{stick}}\to 0$ gives an infinite threshold (no stick, no
slip).

\subsection{Smooth surrogate}

The GPU kernel and theory both use a $C^{\infty}$ harmonic-mean
surrogate.  Let $K = k_{\mathrm{stick}}$ and $M = \mu\,f^{n}_{i}$.
The smooth friction \emph{force magnitude} as a function of
$s = s_i$ is
\begin{equation}
  F^{\mathrm{smooth}}_{\mathrm{fric}}(s)
  \;=\;
  \frac{K\,M\,s}{K\,s + M}
  \;=\;
  \frac{1}{1/K + 1/(M/s)}
  \quad
  (\text{harmonic mean of }K s\text{ and }M),
  \label{eq:friction-smooth}
\end{equation}
and the exact antiderivative is
\begin{equation}
  E^{\mathrm{smooth}}_{\mathrm{fric}}(s)
  \;=\; M\,s \;-\; \frac{M^{2}}{K}\,
        \ln\!\Bigl(1 + \frac{K\,s}{M}\Bigr).
  \label{eq:friction-energy}
\end{equation}
The smoothing zone has half-width $\mu f^{n}/K$ -- by construction the
entire stick range.  Deep stick ($s\to 0$) gives $F\sim K s$; deep
slip ($s\to\infty$) gives the Coulomb plateau $F\to M$.

\paragraph{Kernel implementation form (Phase 4a rewrite).}  The
production Warp kernel implements \eqref{eq:friction-smooth} in the
algebraically equivalent form that avoids $1/s$ entirely:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{scale}_{i}
  \;=\;
  \frac{K\,M}{K\,s_{i} + M + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{denorm}}},
  \qquad
  \bef^{i}_{\mathrm{fric}}
  \;=\; -\,\mathrm{scale}_{i}\,\bdelta_{t,i},
  \label{eq:friction-kernel}
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{denorm}} = 1\times 10^{-30}$ a fp
denormalise-floor that protects the $(K = 0,\ M = 0)$ no-friction
corner.  In any active friction regime $K s + M \gg
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{denorm}}$, so the floor is numerically
invisible.

This form was committed in Phase 4a after the bridge harness
exposed a hidden bias in the legacy kernel form
$\mathrm{inv\_dt\_mag} = s/(s^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})$,
$\mathrm{cone\_scale} = M\cdot\mathrm{inv\_dt\_mag}$,
$\mathrm{scale} = K\,\mathrm{cone\_scale}/(K + \mathrm{cone\_scale} +
\ldots)$.  At production $\varepsilon = 5\times 10^{-4}\,$m and
stick-mode $s \sim \mu$m, $\varepsilon^{2}$ dominated and
$\mathrm{inv\_dt\_mag} \approx s/\varepsilon^{2}$ was $3$--$4$ orders of
magnitude too small -- friction effectively vanished and the kernel
converged to $s = F/k_a$ instead of $s = F/(k_a + k_{\mathrm{stick}})$.
The Phase 4a fix (this rewrite) is algebraically identical to the
contract \eqref{eq:friction-smooth} form everywhere and corrects the
production-scale bias.  \cref{sec:lessons} \#3.

\subsection{Equilibrium with friction (single pad)}

For a single pad face-on flat with anisotropic anchor + external
tangential load $\bef^{\mathrm{ext}}_t$, the normal and tangent axes
decouple and the closed form is the textbook stick/slip law:
\begin{equation}
  \bdelta_t^{*}
  \;=\;
  -\,s^{*}\,\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{t},
  \quad
  \hat{\mathbf{f}}_{t} = \bef^{\mathrm{ext}}_t/\|\bef^{\mathrm{ext}}_t\|,
  \quad
  s^{*}\text{ given by \eqref{eq:stick}--\eqref{eq:slip}}.
  \label{eq:friction-1sphere}
\end{equation}
(The minus sign on $s^{*}\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{t}$ comes from
$\bq = \bp - \bdelta$: with $\bef^{\mathrm{ext}}_t$ in $+\hat{f}_t$,
$\bq$ moves to $+\hat{f}_t$, so $\bdelta_t$ goes to $-s\hat{f}_t$.)
Code: \texttt{equilibrium\_half\_space\_friction\_analytical},
\texttt{...\_hard\_numerical}, \texttt{...\_smooth\_numerical}.
\textbf{Fig.~\ref{fig:ti}}, verified to $1.5\times 10^{-8}$ rel-err
by T-I.

\subsection{Dome grip aggregation (T-J)}
\label{sec:T-J}

For a lattice + flat-face under a uniform applied tangential
displacement $s$ at every active sphere (the indenter-bonded-to-skin
model), the total tangential reaction aggregates the per-sphere
stick--slip law:
\begin{equation}
  F_{\mathrm{grip}}(s)
  \;=\;
  \sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}
  \min\!\bigl(\,K\,s,\ \mu\,f^{n}_{i}\bigr),
  \qquad
  F_{\mathrm{grip}}^{\mathrm{max}}
  \;=\;
  \mu\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}} f^{n}_{i}.
  \label{eq:Fgrip}
\end{equation}
This is a \emph{displacement-driven} aggregation, not a force-driven
equilibrium: it needs no new solver.  Per-sphere $f^{n}_{i}$ is
extracted via \texttt{lattice\_contact\_normal\_forces} from the
normal-only equilibrium $\bdelta^{*}$ produced by
\texttt{solve\_lattice\_contact}, and \eqref{eq:Fgrip} is a closed-form
post-processing step.  The grip plateau matches $\mu\,F^{n,
\mathrm{total}}$ to exact fp precision (the aggregation is
algebraically the spec); the only error budget is the per-sphere
$f^{n}_{i}$ precision inherited from the L-BFGS-B floor of
$\sim 5\times 10^{-8}$.  T-J (\texttt{test\_dome\_grip\_flat.py})
verifies all four parts (baseline, grip-curve invariants,
self-consistency, depth sweep).  \textbf{Fig.~\ref{fig:tj}}.

\section{Emission to the rigid-body solver}
\label{sec:emit}

Once $\bdelta$ has converged, \texttt{write\_cslc\_contacts\_point\_set}
emits one MuJoCo contact per active $(i, j)$ pair, in world frame:
\begin{align}
  \text{point}_0 &\;=\; \bq^{\mathrm{def}}_{i} \;=\; \bp_i - \bdelta_i,
   \label{eq:emit-p0}\\
  \text{point}_1 &\;=\; \bt_j,
   \\
  \text{normal}  &\;=\; -\,\bnf_{\!j}
                 \qquad
                 (\text{from target outward to pad}),
                 \label{eq:emit-normal}\\
  \text{margin}_0 &\;=\; r_i,
   \qquad
   \text{margin}_1 \;=\; 0
   \qquad
   (\text{v2: was }R_j\text{, now zero}),
   \label{eq:emit-margin}\\
  \text{stiffness} &\;=\; k_{\mathrm{series}}\,A_j\,w^{t}_{ij}\,a_{ij}\,
                          \mathrm{gate}_{ij},
   \qquad
   \text{friction}\;=\;\mu.
   \label{eq:emit-stiff}
\end{align}
The downstream solver (MuJoCo) reconstructs the penetration as
\begin{equation}
  \text{solver\_pen}
  \;=\; \text{margin}_0 + \text{margin}_1
        - (\text{point}_1 - \text{point}_0)\!\cdot\!\text{normal}
  \;=\; r_i + 0 - (\bt_j - \bq^{\mathrm{def}}_{i})\!\cdot\!(-\bnf_{\!j})
  \;=\; r_i - \bnf_{\!j}\!\cdot\!(\bq^{\mathrm{def}}_{i} - \bt_j)
  \;=\; \mathrm{raw}_{ij},
  \label{eq:solver-pen}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ exactly the half-space overlap \eqref{eq:raw} the lattice solver
converged on.  The per-contact force MuJoCo applies is therefore
$\text{stiffness}\cdot\text{solver\_pen}
= k_{\mathrm{series}}\,A_j\,w^{t}_{ij}\,a_{ij}\,\mathrm{gate}_{ij}\,
\mathrm{raw}_{ij}$, algebraically the same as
$k_c\,A_j\,w^{t}\,a\,\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}\,\mathrm{gate}$ in the deep
saturated limit (where $\phi^{\mathrm{eff}} \approx \mathrm{raw}$).

\paragraph{Wedge force.}  Because the emitted normal in
\eqref{eq:emit-normal} is the target's outward face normal (a
\emph{rest-target} property), any body-frame pose perturbation that
shifts $\bq^{\mathrm{def}}_i$ relative to $\bt_j$ couples directly
into out-of-plane contact-force components when the face is locally
flat but the pad is curved.  This was a known v1 phenomenon
($\mathrm{notes.md}$ Step 11); v2 inherits it unchanged because the
v1 emitted normal was the deformed line of centres and the v2 normal
is the target face normal -- both are perpendicular to the tangent
plane at the contact patch in the limit of small deformation, and the
wedge interaction is a property of the macroscopic geometry, not the
contact-direction emission.  Resisting the wedge requires friction at
the macroscopic contact (\cref{sec:friction}).

\section{Calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}

The user specifies a target bulk stiffness $k_{e}^{\mathrm{bulk}}$
(the aggregate normal stiffness of the contact patch under face-on
loading).  Under face-on, near-uniform compression with a contact
fraction $\eta\in(0,1)$, the patch supports
$N_{\mathrm{contact}} = \eta\,N_{\mathrm{surface}}$ active pad spheres
in parallel; each sphere is itself a series spring with effective
stiffness $1/k_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1/k_a + 1/k_c + 1/k_{e}$.  The
calibration identity is
\begin{equation}
  N_{\mathrm{contact}}\,k_{\mathrm{eff}}
  \;=\; k_{e}^{\mathrm{bulk}}
  \;\Rightarrow\;
  \frac{1}{k_c}
  \;=\;
  \frac{N_{\mathrm{contact}}}{k_{e}^{\mathrm{bulk}}}
  \;-\; \frac{1}{k_a}
  \;-\; \frac{1}{k_{e}},
  \label{eq:calibration}
\end{equation}
inverted at construction by \texttt{calibrate\_kc}
(\texttt{cslc\_data.py}).  The half-space contact swap does not alter
\eqref{eq:calibration}: $k_c$ is a per-pair stiffness and
$N_{\mathrm{contact}}$ is the count of pad spheres engaged, neither
of which depends on the target radius $R$.

In the kernel, the right-most two springs are composed by a
harmonic-mean with a smoothing regulariser
\begin{equation}
  k_{\mathrm{series}}
  \;=\; \frac{k_c\,k_{e}}{k_c + k_{e} + \varepsilon^{2}},
  \label{eq:series-kernel}
\end{equation}
preserved verbatim from v1 for backward kernel-output compatibility,
with the documented dimensional caveat: $\varepsilon^{2}$ in [m$^{2}$]
is added to $(k_c + k_e)$ in [N$/$m] -- numerically negligible at
production parameters but waiting for a follow-up to give it an
explicitly-typed regulariser.

\paragraph{The five Phase 4 ground rules.}  For every bridge scene:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item $\varepsilon = 5\times 10^{-4}\,$m both sides (production, not
theory-grade $10^{-9}$).  Phase 4 is the first point in the rewrite
where theory runs at production $\varepsilon$.
\item $\mathrm{areas} = \texttt{None}$ on both sides (per-pair Hookean
$k_c$).  Area-weighted hydroelastic calibration is Phase 6+.
\item Theory is the spec.  If a scene fails at the contract bound,
fix the kernel; do not loosen the tolerance.
\item Live $f^{n}$ on both sides.  Phase 3's analytical-decoupled
$f^{n}$ has a $\sim 15$--$20\%$ drift from the smooth equilibrium
$f^{n}$ at production $\varepsilon$; bridge theory uses the
$f^{n}_{i}$ from \texttt{lattice\_contact\_normal\_forces} at the
\emph{smooth} normal equilibrium (\cref{sec:lessons} \#5).
\item Kernel and theory must compute the same gradient.  Phase 4a
dropped $\partial w^{t}/\partial\bdelta$ from \texttt{solve\_lattice\_contact}
(\cref{sec:single-pair}) to match the kernel's truncated
gradient.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Numerical observations and corner cases}
\label{sec:lessons}

The v2 rewrite surfaced 12 findings; each is captured in the contract
\S17 and is a regression guard going forward.  The seven that
materially shape the equations and the harness are summarised below.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{L-BFGS-B floor on multi-sphere problems.}  Convergence
floors at $\sim 5\times 10^{-8}$ rel-err on chain / dome scenes even
with \texttt{eps = 1e-12, gtol = 1e-16}; fp roundoff in the
\texttt{np.add.at} lateral assembly propagates through scipy's
stopping criteria.  Phase 2 and later tolerances are set against this
floor.

\item \emph{Production-eps precision floor.}  At production
$\varepsilon = 5\times 10^{-4}\,$m the smooth-surrogate correction
scales as $\sim 1/(\mathrm{raw}_{\mathrm{eq}}/\varepsilon)^{4}$ in
leading order.  Force matches analytical to $<1\%$ when
$\mathrm{raw}_{\mathrm{eq}} \ge 10\,\varepsilon$.  Below that, the
smoothing dominates and any test must compare against the smooth
fixed point, not the hard-law analytical.

\item \emph{Kernel friction had a hidden bias at production
$\varepsilon$.}  The legacy form
$\mathrm{inv\_dt\_mag} = s/(s^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})$ as a regularised
$1/s$ was only correct at $s \gg \varepsilon$; at production
$\varepsilon$ and stick-mode $s \sim \mu$m, $\varepsilon^{2}$
dominated and friction effectively vanished.  Phase 4a rewrote both
\texttt{jacobi\_step} (v1) and \texttt{jacobi\_step\_point\_set} (v2)
to the contract form \eqref{eq:friction-kernel}, which avoids $1/s$
entirely.  Discovery: T-K scene F kernel gave $s = F/k_a$ while theory
gave $s = F/(k_a + k_{\mathrm{stick}})$ -- a clean $2\times$ ratio that
pinpointed the bug.

\item \emph{Alignment gate insufficient for closed convex side faces.}
The current \eqref{eq:align-gate} smooth band at perpendicular
$\alpha = 0$ gives $a = 0.5$.  Box-shaped targets have flat side faces
that extend perpendicular to the contact-face normal; corner samples
on these side faces sit \emph{inside} the locality kernel and at
$\alpha = 0$, contributing half-strength asymmetric in-plane forces
that prevent L-BFGS-B convergence at production $\varepsilon$.
Phase 4b will shift the band to $[0, +\varepsilon_{\mathrm{align}}]$
(perpendicular hard-culled).  Phase 4a defers the full box target;
scene H is reduced to a single-face multi-sample point set as a
work-around.

\item \emph{Live $f^{n}$ required for bridge parity.}  Contract \S6.4
defines $f^{n}_{i}$ at the iterate's current $\bdelta$ (\emph{live});
Phase 3's \texttt{equilibrium\_half\_space\_friction\_smooth\_numerical}
defaulted to the analytical face-on $f^{n}$ for hard-law decoupling --
exact at $\varepsilon = 10^{-9}\,$m but at production $\varepsilon =
5\times 10^{-4}\,$m the smooth equilibrium $\delta_n$ drifts
$\sim 15$--$20\%$ from analytical (finding \#2 above), and
$f^{n}_{\mathrm{smooth}} \ne f^{n}_{\mathrm{analytical}}$.  This
shifted T-K scene F by $\sim 6\times 10^{-3}$ relative -- kernel and
theory each consistent with themselves but inconsistent with each
other.  Phase 4a added an \texttt{f\_n\_override} kwarg; bridge
harness pre-computes
$f^{n}_{i} = k_a\,|\delta_{n,i,\mathrm{smooth}}|$ from a normal-only
\texttt{solve\_lattice\_contact} and passes it in.  Scene F rel-err
dropped from $6\times 10^{-3}$ to $4\times 10^{-6}$.

\item \emph{$\partial w^{t}/\partial\bdelta$ dropped from theory
solver.}  Phase 2 finding \#4 documented that the theory solver
included this term while the kernel did not.  Phase 4a's
``theory $=$ kernel, never both ways'' rule forced a commit: dropped
from theory.  The term is $O(\varepsilon^{2}/r_i^{3})$, well inside
L-BFGS-B's gradient floor at production scale; all $10$ prior-phase
theory tests pass unchanged after the drop.

\item \emph{T-I $k_{\mathrm{stick}}$ sweep needs $F$-grid spanning
past $\max F_{\mathrm{thresh}}$.}  At $k_{\mathrm{stick}}/k_a = 0.1$,
$F_{\mathrm{thresh}} = 11\,\mu f_n$ -- far past the $6\,\mu f_n$
range that the legacy v1 test\_04 used.  T-I Part D sizes the grid as
$1.5\times F_{\mathrm{thresh}}(\min k_{\mathrm{stick}})$.  Trap to
remember when extending the sweep.
\end{enumerate}
