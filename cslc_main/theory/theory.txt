\begin{abstract}
This document is the theoretical and numerical reference for the CSLC
contact model implemented in \texttt{newton/\_src/geometry/cslc\_kernels.py}.
It states every equation that the kernels are required to satisfy, gives
the closed-form solutions used to verify the kernels, and lists the
verification figures produced by the six theory test drivers
\texttt{cslc\_main/theory/test\_0[1-6]\_*.py} together with the
kernel-to-theory bridge \texttt{test\_07\_kernel\_bridge.py}.
The model itself is built from standard ingredients (Hookean springs,
Hunt--Crossley penalty, regularised Coulomb friction, smoothed positive
parts); the novel content is the way these ingredients are coupled on a
\emph{sphere lattice} so as to recover distributed pressure and
rotational restoring moments while remaining $C^{\infty}$ in the
rigid-body pose.
\end{abstract}

\section{Overview and Scope}
\label{sec:overview}

A robot fingertip is approximated by a rigid body wrapped in a layer
of $n$ surface spheres, the \emph{lattice}.  Each surface sphere
$i$ has a rest centre $\bp_i\in\real^3$, a rest radius $r_i$, an outward
unit rest normal $\bn_i$, and a set of \emph{lattice neighbours}
$\mathcal{N}(i)$ (its first ring of edges in the lattice).  When the
body presses into a rigid target (modelled here as a sphere of centre
$\bt$ and radius $R$), each lattice sphere's centre translates by a
vector displacement $\bdelta_i\in\real^3$, while the rest radius $r_i$
is kept fixed.  The displaced (\emph{deformed}) centre is
\begin{equation}
  \bq_i \;=\; \bp_i \;-\; \bdelta_i.
  \label{eq:def-centre}
\end{equation}
This convention (centre moves, radius does not) is the
\emph{deformed-centre} formulation.  It is the choice that makes the
anchor and the contact springs sit cleanly in series at the level of a
single sphere (\cref{sec:single}) and lets the same kinematics carry
through to lateral coupling, curvature-driven Poisson bulging, and
stick--slip friction without any sign-convention drift between the
modules.\footnote{An earlier formulation, used in the conference
paper~\texttt{cslc\_mujoco/docs/overleaf\_theory\_cslc\_icra.txt}, kept
the centre fixed and reduced the radius instead
($r_i^{\mathrm{eff}}=r_i-\delta_i$).  That choice produces a scalar
$\delta_i\in[0,r_i]$ and a slightly simpler equilibrium, but loses
tangential information and so cannot represent off-axis contact, lateral
shear, or stick--slip via a tangential $\bdelta_t$.}

Three forces act on each lattice sphere:
the \emph{anchor} (\cref{sec:anchor}), pulling $\bq_i$ back toward
$\bp_i$; the \emph{lateral coupling}
(\cref{sec:lateral}), springs that connect $\bq_i$ to its lattice
neighbours; and the \emph{contact} force (\cref{sec:contact}) at sphere
$i$ when its deformed centre overlaps the target.  Stick--slip friction
(\cref{sec:friction}) is added as a fourth term acting in the tangent
plane at the contact patch.  Every gate that would otherwise introduce
non-differentiability is replaced by a $C^{\infty}$ surrogate
parameterised by a single smoothing width $\varepsilon$
(\cref{sec:smoothing}).

\paragraph{Sign conventions.}  Throughout this document, $\delta_n>0$
means the sphere has been compressed \emph{inward} (i.e. $\bq_i$ lies on
the \emph{body} side of $\bp_i$).  Because $\bq_i=\bp_i-\bdelta_i$ the
chain rule contributes a $-1$ when converting between
$\partial/\partial\bq_i$ and $\partial/\partial\bdelta_i$, so the
physical force on the sphere
$\bef^{\mathrm{phys}}_i=-\partial E/\partial\bq_i=+\partial E/\partial\bdelta_i$
coincides in direction with the energy gradient with respect to
$\bdelta_i$.  We therefore work in terms of $\partial E/\partial\bdelta_i$
throughout, and write the equilibrium directly as
$\partial E_{\mathrm{tot}}/\partial\bdelta_i=0$ (sum of gradient
contributions equals zero).  The Warp kernel
(\texttt{cslc\_kernels.py}\,L568, L601) names its per-iteration force
quantities \emph{loads}, defined as $-\partial E_X/\partial\bdelta$;
this is the same physics with the opposite sign convention, and we
flag the sign flip explicitly when we cite the kernel code.  These
conventions are collected in \cref{tab:signs}.

\section{Single sphere: anchor + series-spring contact}
\label{sec:single}

\subsection{Geometry}

Let one lattice sphere face a rigid target sphere of centre $\bt$ and
radius $R$.  The \emph{rest} overlap is
\begin{equation}
  \phi^{\mathrm{rest}}
  \;=\; (r+R) \;-\; \|\bp-\bt\|,
  \label{eq:phi-rest}
\end{equation}
which is positive precisely when the two rest spheres geometrically
overlap.  The \emph{raw} overlap at finite displacement is obtained from
the deformed centre~\eqref{eq:def-centre}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{raw}(\bdelta)
  \;=\; (r+R)\;-\;\|\bt-\bq\|
  \;=\; (r+R)\;-\;\|\bt-\bp+\bdelta\|.
  \label{eq:raw}
\end{equation}
The clamped-positive (\emph{effective}) overlap is
\begin{equation}
  \phi^{\mathrm{eff}}(\bdelta)
  \;=\; [\mathrm{raw}(\bdelta)]_+
  \;=\; \max\!\bigl(0,\;\mathrm{raw}(\bdelta)\bigr),
  \label{eq:phi-eff}
\end{equation}
and the contact direction is the unit vector from the target to the
deformed sphere centre,
\begin{equation}
  \hatu_c(\bdelta)
  \;=\; \frac{\bq-\bt}{\|\bq-\bt\|}
  \;=\; \frac{\bp-\bdelta-\bt}{\|\bp-\bdelta-\bt\|}.
  \label{eq:n-eff-def}
\end{equation}

Code references: \texttt{rest\_overlap}, \texttt{effective\_penetration},
\texttt{contact\_raw\_overlap}, \texttt{contact\_direction}
(\texttt{cslc\_main/theory/cslc\_theory.py}).

\subsection{Forces and energies}

The anchor is a 3-D Hookean spring whose stored energy and gradient
with respect to $\bdelta$ are
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{anchor}}(\bdelta) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,k_a\,\|\bdelta\|^2,
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial E_{\mathrm{anchor}}}{\partial \bdelta} \;=\; k_a\,\bdelta.
  \label{eq:anchor-iso}
\end{equation}
This is Hooke's law on the centre's offset from rest.  $k_a>0$ ensures
coercivity of the total energy.  Code: \texttt{anchor\_force}
(\texttt{cslc\_theory.py}\,L206--221, returns the gradient).

The contact is a Hookean spring on the contact-layer compression
$\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}$:
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{contact}}(\bdelta) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,k_c\,
       \bigl[\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}(\bdelta)\bigr]^{2}.
  \label{eq:contact-energy}
\end{equation}
The chain rule for the gradient picks up an extra $\Sigma_\varepsilon$
factor through $\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}=\sigma_\varepsilon(\mathrm{raw})$;
the full expression is~\eqref{eq:contact-grad} in
\cref{sec:smoothing}.

The total energy
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{tot}}(\bdelta) \;=\; E_{\mathrm{anchor}} \;+\;
                                  E_{\mathrm{contact}}
  \label{eq:E-tot-1sphere}
\end{equation}
is convex on the half-space $\mathrm{raw}>0$ and is bounded below by
zero, so it has a unique minimiser when $k_a>0$.

\subsection{Face-on equilibrium (closed form)}
\label{sec:face-on}

When the target sits exactly on the rest normal,
$\bt-\bp = \|\bt-\bp\|\,\bn$, symmetry forces $\bdelta=\delta_n\bn$ with
$\delta_n\ge 0$, and $\hatu_c=-\bn$.  Writing the scalar equilibrium
$\partial E_{\mathrm{tot}}/\partial\delta_n = 0$,
\begin{equation}
  k_a\,\delta_n \;-\; k_c\,
       \bigl(\phi^{\mathrm{rest}}-\delta_n\bigr)
     \;=\; 0
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \delta_n^{*}
  \;=\;
  \frac{k_c\,\phi^{\mathrm{rest}}}{k_a+k_c},
  \label{eq:1sphere-delta}
\end{equation}
and the magnitude of the resulting contact force is
\begin{equation}
  F^{*} \;=\; \frac{k_a\,k_c}{k_a+k_c}\,\phi^{\mathrm{rest}}
        \;=\; k_{\mathrm{eff}}\,\phi^{\mathrm{rest}},
  \quad
  \frac{1}{k_{\mathrm{eff}}}=\frac{1}{k_a}+\frac{1}{k_c}.
  \label{eq:1sphere-series}
\end{equation}
The combination law~\eqref{eq:1sphere-series} is the classical
\emph{series-spring composition}: the same force flows through the
anchor and the contact spring, while their two compressions add up to
$\phi^{\mathrm{rest}}$.  The rigid-target limit recovers $F^*\to
k_a\,\phi^{\mathrm{rest}}$ as $k_c\to\infty$, and the no-contact limit
gives $F^*\to 0$ as $k_c\to 0$.  Code:
\texttt{equilibrium\_face\_on\_analytical}.

\subsection{Three-body composition}
\label{sec:three-body}

The same logic with a third spring---a compliant target of effective
modulus $k_e$---gives the three-spring series law
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{k_{\mathrm{eff}}}
  \;=\; \frac{1}{k_a} \;+\; \frac{1}{k_c} \;+\; \frac{1}{k_e},
  \label{eq:series-3}
\end{equation}
recovering~\eqref{eq:1sphere-series} when $k_e\to\infty$ (rigid
target).  In the kernel the two right-most springs are combined into
$k_{\mathrm{series}}$ by a harmonic-mean composition with a smoothing
regulariser (cf.~\cref{sec:smoothing}):
\begin{equation}
  k_{\mathrm{series}}
  \;=\; \frac{k_c\,k_e}{k_c+k_e+\varepsilon^{2}},
  \label{eq:series-kernel}
\end{equation}
and the calibration helper
\texttt{calibrate\_kc}\,(\texttt{cslc\_data.py}) inverts~\eqref{eq:series-3}
to obtain $k_c$ from a user-supplied bulk stiffness.

\subsection{Verification (Step 1)}

\texttt{test\_01\_single\_sphere.py} drives four checks.  Numerical
L-BFGS-B on $E_{\mathrm{tot}}(\bdelta)$ matches the closed
form~\eqref{eq:1sphere-delta}--\eqref{eq:1sphere-series} to
$2\times10^{-10}$ relative error across 15 $(\phi,k_c)$ pairs (Part A).
$F^*$ saturates at $k_a\,\phi^{\mathrm{rest}}$ as $k_c\to\infty$ and
vanishes as $k_c\to 0$ (Part B,
\textbf{Fig.~\ref{fig:01b}}).  $F^*$ is linear in $\phi^{\mathrm{rest}}$
with slope $k_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (Part C,
\textbf{Fig.~\ref{fig:01c}}).  For an off-axis target, $\bdelta$ tilts
to follow the contact line while $\|\bdelta\|=750\,\mu$m is conserved
(Part D, \textbf{Fig.~\ref{fig:01d}}).

\section{Lateral coupling between lattice spheres}
\label{sec:lateral}

We now connect spheres into a lattice.  Two laws are implemented side
by side in \texttt{cslc\_lattice.py}; we keep both so the suite can
expose the geometry-blindness of the linear law.

\subsection{Graph-Laplacian law (paper baseline)}

The conference paper writes the lateral force on $\bq_i$ as the
negative discrete Laplacian of the displacement field,
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{lat}}^{\mathrm{GL}}
  \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,k_{\ell}\!\!\!\sum_{(i,j)\in E}\!
        \bigl\|\bdelta_i-\bdelta_j\bigr\|^{2},
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial E_{\mathrm{lat}}^{\mathrm{GL}}}{\partial\bdelta_i}
  \;=\; k_{\ell}\!\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}
        \bigl(\bdelta_i - \bdelta_j\bigr).
  \label{eq:lat-GL}
\end{equation}
(The kernel's load form
$\bef_i^{\mathrm{lat,GL,load}}=-k_{\ell}\sum_j(\bdelta_i-\bdelta_j)$
is the negative gradient; cf.\
\texttt{lateral\_force\_graph\_laplacian} in
\texttt{cslc\_lattice.py}\,L289--306.)  The Hessian
$\partial^2 E^{\mathrm{GL}}_{\mathrm{lat+anchor}}/\partial\bdelta^2$ is
the scalar lattice stiffness matrix
\begin{equation}
  K_{ii} \;=\; k_{a} + k_{\ell}\,|\mathcal{N}(i)|,
  \qquad
  K_{ij} \;=\; -k_{\ell}\;\text{if } j\in\mathcal{N}(i),
  \label{eq:Kmatrix}
\end{equation}
with $\bK\in\real^{n\times n}$ symmetric positive-definite for $k_a>0$.
Code: \texttt{build\_K\_matrix}\,(\texttt{cslc\_lattice.py}\,L185--208).
The full vector system is $(\bK\otimes\bI_3)\bdelta=\bef^{\mathrm{ext}}$,
which decouples per Cartesian axis.

\subsection{Distance-preserving law (ideal)}

The \emph{ideal} lateral law is a Hookean spring on the physical
deformed-centre distance.  The stored energy on edge $(i,j)$ and the
gradient contributions to spheres $i,j$ are
\begin{align}
  E_{\mathrm{lat}}^{\mathrm{DP}}
  &\;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,k_{\ell}\!\!\!\sum_{(i,j)\in E}\!
        \bigl(\|\bq_j-\bq_i\|-L_{ij}\bigr)^{2},
  \qquad L_{ij}\;=\;\|\bp_j-\bp_i\|,
  \label{eq:lat-DP-energy}
  \\
  \frac{\partial E_{\mathrm{lat}}^{\mathrm{DP}}}{\partial\bdelta_i}\bigg|_{\mathrm{edge}\,(i,j)}
  &\;=\; +k_{\ell}\,\bigl(\|\bq_j-\bq_i\|-L_{ij}\bigr)\,
        \hatu_{ij}(\bdelta),
  \quad
  \hatu_{ij}(\bdelta)\;=\;\frac{\bq_j-\bq_i}{\|\bq_j-\bq_i\|},
  \label{eq:lat-DP}
\end{align}
with the opposite sign at sphere $j$ (because
$\partial(\bq_j-\bq_i)/\partial\bdelta_j=-\bI$, the discrete analogue of
Newton's third law for the spring tension).  Code:
\texttt{lateral\_energy\_distance\_preserving},
\texttt{lateral\_force\_distance\_preserving}\\
(\texttt{cslc\_lattice.py}\,L318--363).\footnote{The
\texttt{lateral\_force\_distance\_preserving} helper returns the
\emph{gradient} (not the load), while its sibling
\texttt{lateral\_force\_graph\_laplacian} returns the load
($-$gradient).  These two helpers therefore disagree on sign by a
factor of $-1$; this was the gradient-sign bug listed in
\texttt{notes.md} (lesson \#2), fixed in
\texttt{solve\_lattice\_contact\_numerical} by writing the gradient
inline rather than re-using the helpers.}

Linearising~\eqref{eq:lat-DP} about $\bdelta=0$, the distance-preserving
gradient on edge $(i,j)$ collapses to the rank-1 projector
$\hatu_{ij}^{\mathrm{rest}}\otimes\hatu_{ij}^{\mathrm{rest}}$ acting on
$(\bdelta_i-\bdelta_j)$, so it agrees with the graph
Laplacian~\eqref{eq:lat-GL} only on the rest-edge direction; it does
\emph{not} resist transverse load.  The two laws therefore diverge as
soon as the displacement has a component perpendicular to a rest edge,
as we make precise in Steps 2--5.

\subsection{Chain spectrum and Green's function (Step 2)}

Specialising~\eqref{eq:Kmatrix} to an $N$-sphere chain (degree 1 at the
ends, degree 2 in the interior), $\bK$ is tridiagonal and its
eigenvalues are the discrete-Neumann spectrum
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_k \;=\; k_a \;+\; 2k_{\ell}
                  \bigl(1-\cos(k\pi/N)\bigr),
  \qquad k=0,1,\ldots,N-1.
  \label{eq:chain-spectrum}
\end{equation}
The mode $k=0$ has eigenvalue $\lambda_0=k_a$: uniform translation, the
only mode the lateral spring cannot resist.  The chain's discrete
Green's function $g_i$ for a point source at $i=0$ decays as
$g_i\propto z_-^{|i|}$ with
\begin{equation}
  z_- \;=\; (1+\alpha) \;-\;
            \sqrt{\alpha(\alpha+2)},
  \qquad
  \alpha = \frac{k_a}{2k_\ell},
  \qquad
  \ell_c^{\mathrm{disc}} = -\,\frac{1}{\ln z_-}.
  \label{eq:chain-decay}
\end{equation}
The continuum approximation $\ell_c=\sqrt{k_\ell/k_a}$ is exact only in
the $k_\ell\gg k_a$ limit; at the production default $k_\ell/k_a=0.2$,
the discrete decay length is $0.520$ spacings, not the continuum
$0.447$ (\textbf{Fig.~\ref{fig:02c}}).  Code:
\texttt{chain\_discrete\_decay\_length},
\texttt{chain\_analytical\_eigenvalues}.

\subsection{Chain $+$ contact (Step 3)}

Adding a single contact target above sphere $k$ inserts a
$+k_c\,e_k e_k^\top$ rank-1 update on the normal axis.
Sherman--Morrison gives the closed form
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{n,k} \;=\; \frac{k_c\,\phi^{\mathrm{rest}}\,g_{kk}}
                          {1+k_c\,g_{kk}},
  \qquad g_{kk} = (\bK^{-1})_{kk},
  \label{eq:chain-contact}
\end{equation}
i.e. the single-sphere series-spring formula~\eqref{eq:1sphere-delta}
with $k_a$ replaced by the lattice-effective $k_a^{\mathrm{eff}}=
1/g_{kk}$.  Lateral coupling \emph{stiffens} the centre's response
because neighbours share the load
(\textbf{Fig.~\ref{fig:03a}--\ref{fig:03b}}).  Force balance and the
chain decay follow the Green's function~\eqref{eq:chain-decay}, and the
total reaction $\sum_i F_i=k_c(\phi^{\mathrm{rest}}-\delta_{n,k})$
recovers the contact force (Newton III).

On a 1-D chain with distance-preserving lateral the
\emph{perpendicular} bulge is third-order in $\delta$ (each lateral
spring is stretched by $\sim\delta^2/(2h)$, with a transverse
projection $\sim\delta/h$, giving a transverse force
$\sim k_\ell\,\delta^{3}/(2h^{2})$): the chain is too flat to bulge
outward.  Outward bulging requires curvature; see~\cref{sec:arc}.

\section{Curvature unlocks outward bulging (Step 5)}
\label{sec:arc}

A curved lattice is what lets neighbours bulge \emph{outward} (away
from the body) when a central sphere is pressed inward.  Consider an
arc of $N$ spheres on a circle of radius $R_{\mathrm{pad}}$ with
arc-length spacing $h$.  The angular spacing is $\alpha=h/R_{\mathrm{pad}}$,
each sphere's outward normal is radial, and the right-neighbour edge
vector at the apex is, to second order in $\alpha$,
$\bp_{r}-\bp_{0}=(R_{\mathrm{pad}}\alpha,\,-R_{\mathrm{pad}}\alpha^{2}/2,\,0)$.
Compressing the apex by $\delta_y$ along $+y$ (so $\bq_0$ moves
inward by $\delta_y$) gives a deformed edge
\begin{equation}
  \bq_{r}-\bq_{0}
  \;=\;
  \bigl(R_{\mathrm{pad}}\alpha,\;
        \delta_y - R_{\mathrm{pad}}\alpha^{2}/2,\;0\bigr),
  \label{eq:arc-edge}
\end{equation}
whose squared length satisfies
\begin{equation}
  L_{\mathrm{def}}^{2} - L_{\mathrm{rest}}^{2}
   \;=\;
   \delta_y\,\bigl(\delta_y - R_{\mathrm{pad}}\alpha^{2}\bigr).
  \label{eq:arc-window}
\end{equation}
The lateral spring is therefore \emph{compressed}
($L_{\mathrm{def}}<L_{\mathrm{rest}}$) precisely in the window
$0<\delta_y<R_{\mathrm{pad}}\alpha^{2}$, and a compressed spring pushes
its endpoints apart---which means the perimeter neighbour is pushed
\emph{outward} along its own radial normal: local $\delta_n<0$, i.e.\
an \emph{outward bulge}.  Beyond
$\delta_y=R_{\mathrm{pad}}\alpha^{2}$ the spring is stretched again,
and the chain story (same-sign neighbours) returns.  The graph-Laplacian
law~\eqref{eq:lat-GL} cannot reproduce this window: it sees only
$\bdelta_i-\bdelta_j$, with no awareness of the edge direction
geometry, and therefore always spreads load with the same sign.

\section{Anisotropic anchor (Step 6)}
\label{sec:anisotropic}

Real skin is closer to an incompressible solid than to an isotropic
spring.  We replace the isotropic anchor~\eqref{eq:anchor-iso} by a
per-axis decomposition in the sphere's local
$\{\bn,\bn^{\perp}\}$ frame:
\begin{align}
  E_{\mathrm{anchor}}
  &\;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,k_a\,\delta_n^{2}
       + \tfrac{1}{2}\,(k_a\,\rho)\,\|\bdelta_t\|^{2},
  \label{eq:anchor-aniso}\\
  \bef_a^{(\mathrm{load})}
  &\;=\; k_a\,\delta_n\,\bn + (k_a\rho)\,\bdelta_t,
  \quad
  \delta_n=\bdelta\!\cdot\!\bn,
  \quad
  \bdelta_t=\bdelta-\delta_n\bn,
  \label{eq:anchor-aniso-force}
\end{align}
with the tangent ratio $\rho=k_{a,t}/k_a$.  An isotropic linear-elastic
medium with Poisson ratio $\nu$ satisfies
$\rho=1/(2(1+\nu))$, so the incompressible limit $\nu\to 1/2$ gives
$\rho\to 1/3$.  $\rho=1$ recovers the isotropic Step-1
formulation.

The off-axis equilibrium at small target offset angle $\theta$ from the
normal direction yields the exact tilt ratio
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\delta_t}{\delta_n}
  \;=\; \frac{k_a - A}{k_a\rho - A}\,\tan\theta,
  \qquad
  A \;=\; \frac{k_c\,\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}}{L},
  \label{eq:aniso-tilt}
\end{equation}
where $L=\|\bq-\bt\|$ at equilibrium and $A$ is the effective contact
spring's tangent-plane coupling.  The naive linear form
$(k_a/k_{a,t})\tan\theta$ drops $A$; at $\rho=1/3$ that error is
$\sim 3\%$, at $\rho=0.1$ it is $\sim 14\%$.  The test driver
\texttt{test\_06\_anisotropic\_anchor.py} verifies the exact
formula~\eqref{eq:aniso-tilt} to $6\times 10^{-8}$ relative error.

\section{Stick--slip friction (Step 4 \& 6)}
\label{sec:friction}

Friction is added as a fourth force acting purely in the contact
plane.  In \emph{stick} mode the contact patch is treated as bonded to
the target, with a tangent spring of stiffness $k_{\mathrm{stick}}$
restoring $\bq$ toward the bonded point.  In \emph{slip} mode the force
saturates at the Coulomb cone $\mu\,f_n$.  In the local tangent frame
let $s=\|\bdelta_t\|$ and $F=\|\bef_t^{\mathrm{ext}}\|$ be the applied
tangential load magnitudes; $f_n$ is the equilibrium normal force from
\eqref{eq:1sphere-series}.

\subsection{Hard (piecewise) law}

The hard piecewise stick--slip law is two parallel springs
($k_{a,t}=k_a\rho$ from~\eqref{eq:anchor-aniso} and $k_{\mathrm{stick}}$)
clamped at the cone:
\begin{align}
  \text{stick: }\quad s
  &= \frac{F}{k_{a,t}+k_{\mathrm{stick}}},
  & F_{\mathrm{fric}} &= k_{\mathrm{stick}}\,s,
  & \text{if}\ k_{\mathrm{stick}}\,s \le \mu f_n,
  \label{eq:stick}\\
  \text{slip: }\quad s
  &= \frac{F - \mu f_n}{k_{a,t}},
  & F_{\mathrm{fric}} &= \mu f_n,
  & \text{otherwise,}
  \label{eq:slip}\\
  F_{\mathrm{thresh}}
  &= \mu f_n\,
     \frac{k_{a,t}+k_{\mathrm{stick}}}{k_{\mathrm{stick}}}.
  & & &
  \label{eq:Fthresh}
\end{align}
Limits: $k_{\mathrm{stick}}\to\infty$ recovers bare Coulomb
($F_{\mathrm{thresh}}\to\mu f_n$); $k_{\mathrm{stick}}\to 0$ gives an
infinite threshold (no stick, no slip).  The dimensionless ratio
$\rho$ enters via $k_{a,t}=k_a\rho$.

\subsection{Smooth (kernel) law}

Inside the GPU kernel a $C^{\infty}$ surrogate replaces the hard
piecewise law.  Letting $K=k_{\mathrm{stick}}$ and $M=\mu f_n$,
\begin{equation}
  F_{\mathrm{fric}}^{\mathrm{smooth}}(s)
  \;=\; \frac{K\,M\,s}{K\,s + M}
  \;=\; \frac{1}{1/K + 1/(M/s)}
  \quad(\text{harmonic mean of } K\,s\ \text{and}\ M),
  \label{eq:friction-smooth}
\end{equation}
whose exact antiderivative is
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{fric}}^{\mathrm{smooth}}(s)
  \;=\; M\,s \;-\;
        \frac{M^{2}}{K}\,
        \ln\!\Bigl(1+\tfrac{K\,s}{M}\Bigr).
  \label{eq:friction-energy}
\end{equation}
The smoothing zone has half-width $\mu f_n / k_{\mathrm{stick}}$ -- by
construction the entire stick range.  Deep stick ($s\to 0$) recovers
$F\sim K\,s$, deep slip ($s\to\infty$) recovers the Coulomb plateau
$F\to M$ (\textbf{Fig.~\ref{fig:04c}--\ref{fig:04d}}).  Code:
\texttt{friction\_force\_smooth},\\\texttt{friction\_energy\_smooth},
\texttt{equilibrium\_with\_friction\_*}.

\section{Smoothing for differentiability}
\label{sec:smoothing}

All hard gates in the model -- the positive-part clamp in
$\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}$~\eqref{eq:phi-eff}, the contact-active gate, the
Coulomb kink in the friction law, and the harmonic-mean composition
\eqref{eq:series-kernel} -- are replaced by $C^{\infty}$ surrogates so
that gradients of the contact wrench with respect to rigid-body pose
exist and can be propagated by reverse-mode autodiff.  Define
\begin{align}
  \sigma_{\varepsilon}(x)
  &\;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(x + \sqrt{x^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\bigr),
  \quad &\text{(smooth ReLU)},
  \label{eq:sigma}\\
  \Sigma_{\varepsilon}(x)
  &\;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(1 + \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}}\right),
  \quad &\text{(smooth step / Heaviside)}.
  \label{eq:Sigma}
\end{align}
Both are infinitely differentiable for $\varepsilon>0$, recover their
hard counterparts as $\varepsilon\to 0$ with pointwise error bounded by
$\varepsilon/2$, and satisfy the chain-rule identity
$\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}=\Sigma_{\varepsilon}$.  The effective
overlap and active-gate are
\begin{equation}
  \phi^{\mathrm{eff}}(\bdelta)
  \;=\; \sigma_{\varepsilon}\!\bigl(\mathrm{raw}(\bdelta)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \text{gate}(\bdelta)
  \;=\; \Sigma_{\varepsilon}\!\bigl(\mathrm{raw}(\bdelta)\bigr).
  \label{eq:phi-smooth}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The silent gradient bug.}  Because $\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}=
\sigma_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{raw})$, the gradient of the contact
energy~\eqref{eq:contact-energy} picks up a chain-rule factor:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial E_{\mathrm{contact}}}{\partial\bdelta}
  \;=\; k_c\,\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}\,
        \Sigma_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{raw})\,
        \hatu_c,
  \label{eq:contact-grad}
\end{equation}
not $k_c\,\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}\,\hatu_c$ on its own.  Forgetting
$\Sigma_{\varepsilon}$ is invisible in deep saturated contact
($\mathrm{raw}\gg\varepsilon$, $\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\approx 1$) but is
up to a factor of 2 wrong at contact onset
($\mathrm{raw}\sim\varepsilon$), and on non-contact spheres
($\mathrm{raw}\ll-\varepsilon$) it leaks a spurious
$k_c\,\varepsilon/2$ load because $\sigma_{\varepsilon}$ has an
irreducible $\varepsilon/2$ floor while $\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\to 0$ kills
the leak.  This is the kernel's Step-7 fix
(\texttt{jacobi\_step}, \texttt{cslc\_kernels.py}\,L555--568): the
contact load is written
$k_c\,\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}\,\Sigma_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{raw})\,
\hatu_c$, with the gate factor explicit.

\paragraph{Default smoothing width.}  $\varepsilon=10^{-5}\,$m makes
the gate effectively binary above $0.1\,$mm of penetration
($|\Sigma_{\varepsilon}-1|<10^{-9}$) while preserving $C^{\infty}$
through the threshold, and the steady-state equilibrium shifts by less
than $\varepsilon/2$ -- below the rigid-body solver's precision.

\section{Lattice quasistatic equilibrium}
\label{sec:lattice-eq}

The total energy on the lattice is
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{tot}}(\bdelta_{1\!:\!N})
  \;=\; \sum_{i} E_{\mathrm{anchor}}(\bdelta_i)
  \;+\; E_{\mathrm{lat}}(\bdelta_{1\!:\!N})
  \;+\; \sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}} E_{\mathrm{contact}}^{(i)}(\bdelta_i)
  \;+\; \sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}} E_{\mathrm{fric}}^{(i)}(\bdelta_i),
  \label{eq:E-tot}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{S}$ is the set of surface spheres in contact and
$E_{\mathrm{lat}}$ is the lateral energy
\eqref{eq:lat-DP-energy}.  The quasistatic equilibrium is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial E_{\mathrm{tot}}}{\partial \bdelta_i}\bigg|_{i}
  \;=\; 0,
  \qquad \forall i,
  \label{eq:eq-cond}
\end{equation}
which expands per sphere to the gradient-sum balance
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{k_a\,\bdelta_i^{(\mathrm{aniso})}}_{\partial E_a/\partial\bdelta_i}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}
        k_{\ell}\bigl(\|\bq_j-\bq_i\|-L_{ij}\bigr)\hatu_{ij}}_{\partial E_{\mathrm{lat}}^{\mathrm{DP}}/\partial\bdelta_i}
  \;+\; \underbrace{k_c\,\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}_i\,
          \Sigma_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{raw}_i)\,\hatu_{c,i}}_{\partial E_c/\partial\bdelta_i}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\partial E_{\mathrm{fric}}^{(i)}/\partial\bdelta_i}_{\text{see~\eqref{eq:friction-smooth}}}
  \;=\; 0,
  \label{eq:eq-perspheres}
\end{equation}
with $\bdelta_i^{(\mathrm{aniso})} = \delta_{n,i}\bn_i +
\rho\,\bdelta_{t,i}$ for the anisotropic anchor of
\cref{sec:anisotropic} ($\rho=1$ recovers the isotropic case).  Every
term carries a $+$ sign because we have written the equilibrium as
``sum of gradients $=0$''; the kernel implements the algebraically
equivalent form
$k_a\,\bdelta = \bef_{\mathrm{lat}}^{\mathrm{load}} +
\bef_c^{\mathrm{load}} + \bef_{\mathrm{fric}}^{\mathrm{load}}$
with each $\bef^{\mathrm{load}}_X = -\partial E_X/\partial\bdelta$, which
is why the kernel's $\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{eff}}=-\hatu_c$ and its
\texttt{f\_lateral} carries the opposite sign of~\eqref{eq:lat-DP}.

\subsection{Lattice stiffness matrix}

Linearising the system about $\bdelta=0$ gives the scalar lattice
stiffness matrix $\bK$~\eqref{eq:Kmatrix}.  For the closed-form
warm-start, the kernel actually assembles two per-axis variants of $\bK$
in the sphere's local $\{\bn,\bn^\perp\}$ frame (\texttt{cslc\_data.py}\,L437--465):
\begin{equation}
  \bK_n \;=\; \bK \;+\; k_c\bI
  \quad\bigl(\text{normal axis, contact spring on every diagonal}\bigr),
  \qquad
  \bK_t \;=\; (k_a\rho)\,\bI \;+\; k_\ell\,L_{\mathrm{graph}}
  \quad\bigl(\text{tangent axis, no contact spring}\bigr),
  \label{eq:Kn-Kt}
\end{equation}
where $L_{\mathrm{graph}}=\mathrm{diag}(|\mathcal{N}(i)|)-A$ is the
unweighted graph Laplacian (degree minus adjacency).  Both $\bK_n$ and
$\bK_t$ are SPD for $k_a>0$ and $\rho>0$, and are inverted once at
construction.  In the saturated face-on limit all per-sphere outward
normals are parallel and the kernel's contact-load vector projects
entirely on the normal axis, so the only non-trivial axis is the
normal one; in the curved / off-axis case the tangent axis carries the
shear deflection.

\subsection{Solver: warm-start $+$ damped Jacobi}

The kernel solves~\eqref{eq:eq-perspheres} in two stages.
\paragraph{Linear warm-start.}  At $\bdelta=0$ the contact load on
sphere $j$ is $k_c\,\phi^{\mathrm{rest}}_j\,\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{eff},j}^{\mathrm{rest}}$
(in the kernel's load form, with
$\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{rest}}=(\bt-\bp)/\|\bt-\bp\|$).  The
kernel \texttt{lattice\_solve\_equilibrium}
(\texttt{cslc\_kernels.py}\,L188--276) projects this load onto each
sphere's local $\{\bn_j,\bn_j^\perp\}$ frame and solves the per-axis
linear systems
\begin{align}
  \bK_n\,\delta_n
  &\;=\; k_c\,\bm{f}_n
  \qquad
  \text{with}\quad
  f_{n,j}\;=\;\phi^{\mathrm{rest}}_j\,\bigl(\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{eff},j}^{\mathrm{rest}}\!\cdot\!\bn_j\bigr),
  \label{eq:warmstart-n}
  \\
  \bK_t\,\bdelta_t
  &\;=\; k_c\,\mathbf{f}_t
  \qquad
  \text{with}\quad
  \mathbf{f}_{t,j}\;=\;\phi^{\mathrm{rest}}_j\,\bigl(\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{eff},j}^{\mathrm{rest}}-(\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{eff},j}^{\mathrm{rest}}\!\cdot\!\bn_j)\bn_j\bigr),
  \label{eq:warmstart-t}
\end{align}
in closed form via the precomputed dense inverses $\bK_n^{-1},
\bK_t^{-1}$.  For face-on contact
$\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{eff},j}^{\mathrm{rest}}\parallel\bn_j$ so
$\mathbf{f}_t=0$ and~\eqref{eq:warmstart-n} reduces to the scalar
$(\bK + k_c\bI)\delta_n=k_c\,\bm{\phi}^{\mathrm{rest}}$ -- the
Sherman--Morrison form used in
\cref{sec:lateral}'s chain analysis.  The $O(n^2)$ matvec is exact in
the small-$\bdelta$ limit, absorbs 10--20 Jacobi iterations of
refinement in production scenes, and preserves \texttt{wp.Tape}
backward through the lattice solve for gradient-based downstream uses
(MPC, RL).

\paragraph{Damped Jacobi refinement.}  The nonlinear residual of
\eqref{eq:eq-perspheres} is removed by a Picard iteration with implicit
diagonal stabilisation.  Splitting $\bdelta$ into local normal and
tangent components $(\delta_n,\bdelta_t)$ in each sphere's rest frame,
\begin{align}
  (k_a + S_n)\,\delta_{n,i}^{(k+1)}
  &\;=\; \mathrm{rhs}_{n,i}
       \;+\; S_n\,\delta_{n,i}^{(k)},
  \\
  (k_a\rho + S_t)\,\bdelta_{t,i}^{(k+1)}
  &\;=\; \mathrm{rhs}_{t,i}
       \;+\; S_t\,\bdelta_{t,i}^{(k)},
\end{align}
where $S_n=k_\ell|\mathcal{N}(i)|+k_c\,\mathrm{gate}_i$ and
$S_t=k_\ell|\mathcal{N}(i)|$ are diagonal contraction bounds on
the linearised lateral $+$ contact operators, and
$\mathrm{rhs}$ is the explicit per-iteration residual (lateral $+$
contact $+$ friction $+$ external load).  The update is damped by
$\alpha\in(0,1)$:
\begin{equation}
  \bdelta_i^{(k+1)}
  \;\leftarrow\;
   (1-\alpha)\,\bdelta_i^{(k)}
   + \alpha\,\bdelta_i^{\mathrm{Jacobi}}.
  \label{eq:jacobi-damp}
\end{equation}
$\alpha=0.3$ converges in 40--100 sweeps on production grasp scenes;
sharp contacts and near-slip friction take up to $\sim 2000$.

\section{Emission to the rigid-body solver}
\label{sec:emit}

Once $\bdelta$ has converged, \texttt{write\_cslc\_contacts} writes
one rigid contact per surface sphere with the following data, in
world frame:
\begin{align}
  \text{point}_0 &\;=\; \bq^{\mathrm{def}}\;=\;\bp - \bdelta,
   \label{eq:emit-p0}\\
  \text{point}_1 &\;=\; \bt,
   \\
  \text{normal}  &\;=\; \frac{\bt - \bq^{\mathrm{def}}}
                              {\|\bt - \bq^{\mathrm{def}}\|},
                              \label{eq:emit-normal}\\
  \text{margin}_0 &\;=\; r,
   \qquad
   \text{margin}_1 \;=\; R,
   \label{eq:emit-margin}\\
  \text{stiffness} &\;=\; k_{\mathrm{series}}\,\Sigma_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{raw}),
   \qquad
   \text{friction}\;=\;1.
   \label{eq:emit-stiff}
\end{align}
The downstream solver (MuJoCo) reconstructs
\begin{equation}
  \text{solver\_pen}
  \;=\; \text{margin}_0 + \text{margin}_1
        - (\text{point}_1 - \text{point}_0)\!\cdot\!\text{normal}
  \;=\; (r+R) - \|\bt-\bq^{\mathrm{def}}\|
  \;=\; \mathrm{raw},
  \label{eq:solver-pen}
\end{equation}
i.e. exactly the deformed overlap that \texttt{jacobi\_step} converges
on.  The per-contact force MuJoCo applies is therefore
$\text{stiffness}\cdot\text{solver\_pen}=
k_{\mathrm{series}}\cdot\Sigma_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{raw})\cdot
\mathrm{raw}$ -- the same series-spring law the kernel solved.

\section{Calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}

The user specifies a target bulk stiffness $k_e^{\mathrm{bulk}}$ (the
aggregate normal stiffness of the contact patch under face-on
loading).  Under face-on, near-uniform compression with a contact
fraction $\eta\in(0,1)$, the patch supports
$N_{\mathrm{contact}}=\eta\,N_{\mathrm{surface}}$ active spheres in
parallel; each sphere is itself a series spring with effective
stiffness $1/k_{\mathrm{eff}}=1/k_a+1/k_c+1/k_e$
(\cref{sec:three-body}).  The calibration identity is therefore
\begin{equation}
  N_{\mathrm{contact}}\,k_{\mathrm{eff}}
  \;=\; k_e^{\mathrm{bulk}}
  \;\Rightarrow\;
  \frac{1}{k_c}
  \;=\;
  \frac{N_{\mathrm{contact}}}{k_e^{\mathrm{bulk}}}
  \;-\; \frac{1}{k_a}
  \;-\; \frac{1}{k_e},
  \label{eq:calibration}
\end{equation}
which is inverted at construction by
\texttt{calibrate\_kc}\,(\texttt{cslc\_data.py}).  In the rigid-target
limit ($k_e\to\infty$) the third term drops, recovering the two-spring
identity.  Negative $1/k_c$ (anchor or target too soft to reach
$k_e^{\mathrm{bulk}}$) triggers the conservative fallback
$k_c=k_e^{\mathrm{bulk}}/N_{\mathrm{contact}}$.

\section{Numerical observations and lessons}
\label{sec:lessons}

Six issues were uncovered while writing the gold-reference suite; each
is now a regression guard.  Listed for completeness because they
informed several of the equations above.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item Discrete vs continuum decay length: the continuum
$\ell_c=\sqrt{k_\ell/k_a}$ is wrong at sub-grid coupling.  Use the
discrete formula~\eqref{eq:chain-decay} below $k_\ell\gg k_a$.

\item Gradient sign drift: any time two helpers can disagree on whether
they return $\partial E/\partial\bdelta$ or $-\partial E/\partial\bdelta$,
write the gradient inline; add FD vs analytical agreement as a unit
test.

\item Linear approximation at non-contact spheres:
$\mathrm{raw}\approx\phi^{\mathrm{rest}}-\bdelta\!\cdot\!\bn$ collapses
to 0 (because kernel 1 already clipped $\phi^{\mathrm{rest}}$), and the
smooth-ReLU floor $\varepsilon/2$ leaks a $k_c\varepsilon/2$ contact
force.  Fix: compute $\mathrm{raw}$ from the exact deformed
overlap~\eqref{eq:raw}, which is strongly negative away from contact.

\item The smooth-step chain-rule factor
$\Sigma_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{raw})$ in~\eqref{eq:contact-grad} is
invisible at deep saturated contact, up to 2$\times$ wrong at contact
onset, and sign-flips the arc bulge in test\_07 scene D when omitted.

\item External-load sign: with $\bq=\bp-\bdelta$, an external force
$\bef^{\mathrm{ext}}$ applied to $\bq$ has potential
$V=+\bef^{\mathrm{ext}}\!\cdot\!\bdelta$, so its load on $\bdelta$ is
$-\bef^{\mathrm{ext}}$.  Magnitude-only test criteria mask this; assert
on the vector.

\item Anisotropic anchor on tangent dynamics: any line in a friction
solver that writes $k_a$ on a tangent axis is a latent bug that fires
the first time $\rho\ne 1$.  The fix in all three solvers
(analytical, hard, smooth) is to use $k_{a,t}=k_a\rho$ uniformly.
\end{enumerate}
